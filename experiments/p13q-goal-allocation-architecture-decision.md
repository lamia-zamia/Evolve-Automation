# P13Q — Goal-allocation architecture decision

## Decision: B — retire the current experimental selector

The per-Market-tick selector is test-only and has no demonstrated autonomous-allocation benefit that justifies retaining a full native Research catalog evaluation and prerequisite graph construction on every Market tick. P13N did not isolate a safely removable CPU cost; P13O showed the bounded renderer CPU protocol cannot resolve 2%; and P13P avoided zero of 125 graph evaluations. The available evidence does not support promoting this planner to production or continuing to tune its prefilter.

The canonical autonomous-allocation plan needs an owner that coordinates Research, production, construction, storage, and imports against downstream prestige progress. This selector is only a Stage 4A Research-to-Market hint: it runs in a test hook, can request one bounded current-cycle demand, and does not establish cross-owner autonomous allocation. Retiring this experiment does not resolve that broader plan; it prevents an unproven Market-only planner from becoming its proxy. Revisit autonomous allocation as a distinct owner-level design with explicit native observation ownership and a measured benefit case.

No production integration is authorized, and this is not a CPU-readiness decision.

## Current cycle dataflow and ownership

The captured runtime increments the cycle and resets mutation/freshness state, then performs early demand-control preparation and any enabled construction/project/trigger discovery. The relevant order is:

1. **Pre-cycle and demand preparation.** `progression.beginProcessedCycle()` starts the cycle. Demand prerequisites and enabled construction, project, trigger, and fleet catalogs are prepared before their consumers.
2. **Gather-side claim work.** When the test surface enables a goal-derived claim intervention, the runtime samples offered Research controls and calls `refreshResourceClaim()` before Gather. That test-only planner may construct a MAD prerequisite graph from current root, offers, definitions, grant levels, applicability, queue state, and route/progression identity. Gather then reads demand and mutates resources.
3. **Market.** If enabled, Market controls are ensured and discovered settings refreshed, then `marketAutomation.run()` executes. The Stage 4A hook is attached to the captured trade-route test surface. It receives the current Market route input/context and calls `readCurrentResearchCatalog()` from the hook to obtain current offers, definitions, grants, applicability, resource samples, root/progression generations, Research permission/trigger/queue state, and offer control generations. The experimental pure selector normalizes the catalog, builds its own MAD prerequisite graph, evaluates goal candidates and current Research shortfalls, then joins a candidate to current Market route/resource/control evidence. This graph evaluation is test-only; native Market route planning/execution remains the production owner.
4. **Middle phases.** Hell, Galaxy Market, mining, and other enabled owners run after Market. They may mutate resources or progression. Their mutations invalidate dependent cycle evidence where the owner records them.
5. **Native Research.** Near the later progression section, if triggers are inactive and `autoResearch` is enabled, `progression.runResearchCycle()` performs the native Research owner’s decision and action. It checks progression epoch around execution and invalidates demand authority after a mutation. The Stage 4A test selector does not own or replace this action.
6. **Later automation and MAD performance diagnostics.** Power, prestige and later features execute. At the end, only when MAD is selected and performance diagnostics are enabled, a diagnostic builds/reads the MAD prerequisite graph cache. This occurs after Market, so it cannot supply the earlier selector during the same cycle.
7. **Cycle finalization.** The test surface may refresh the resource claim after progression-mutating phases. That late refresh can build another test-only claim graph. Planning panels and state-log work follow.

### Mutation and freshness boundaries

The root object identity, root generation and progression epoch are distinct authorities. A progression mutation changes the epoch and expires dependent demand observations; root replacement changes root identity/generation. Offered action generations, definitions/effective prerequisite identity, grants, applicability and MAD control generation also affect the cached diagnostic graph identity. Market route/board and controls have their own current capture/generation fence. Resource holding/capacity can change without changing a prerequisite graph, but it changes whether a current demand can be emitted and accepted. Gather and Market themselves mutate resource holdings; Research and other progression phases can alter offers, controls and prices. The post-Research epoch check explicitly invalidates demand after progression changes.

The late MAD diagnostic cache is therefore not a safe substitute for a Market-time selector graph, and the pre-Gather resource-claim graph is not automatically reusable either: it is test-only, may be based on a different effective-prerequisite source, is computed for a different consumer, and later Gather mutations occur before Market. No cross-cycle selector cache has complete authority over all of these invalidation inputs.

## P13P: what `unknown` means

The existing selector can emit `status: "signal"` only when a selected current Research candidate has a current shortfall for a resource with a matching current Market resource observation, a valid route row, and authorized native Market control. It also requires the current native Research catalog/offer authority and owner settings. In the current-cycle output-equivalence question, an absent Market observation is not an invitation to assume a future observation: it means that resource cannot be signaled by the current selector from that capture.

P13P's gate instead treated missing Market rows as `unknown` because it required enough evidence to establish that no Market signal might be possible, while stopping after the first cost-to-Market join. That is stronger than needed for equivalence with the existing selector's current-cycle output. A missing row could be a sufficient negative for a candidate resource if the Market capture is complete and current, the relevant route/control join is authoritative, and every candidate path that could otherwise signal is covered. But the retained per-call data does not prove that coverage.

Interpretation: **C — a mixture.** Missing Knowledge/Mana observations are genuine absent evidence for those specific current Market joins, and therefore block signals for those resources on that call. P13P also demanded a future-impossibility style proof before rejecting a call, and did not retain enough whole-capture evidence to show that the first missing row excludes all alternate candidate/resource combinations. Explicit exclusion, incomplete capture, and inconsistent capture must remain distinct:

- **Absent observation:** no matching resource row exists in the current Market capture. It can rule out a current signal for that resource only when capture completeness and route/control authority are current.
- **Explicit exclusion:** an observed row/route/control expressly says locked, disabled, disallowed, or lacks the authorized method. That is an authoritative current negative for the corresponding candidate/resource join.
- **Incomplete capture:** `captureFailures` or missing owner/root/board/catalog completeness makes absence ambiguous; it cannot safely stand in for a negative.
- **Inconsistent capture:** contradictory Research and Market holdings, generations or identities remain unknown; do not choose one owner’s value as a proxy.

### Quantified selectivity boundary

P13P retained 125 calls: 4 stopped at missing Knowledge and 121 at missing Mana; it examined 1 cost entry and 14 Market rows per call (1,875 deterministic record checks), returned `unknown` 125 times, and avoided 0 graph constructions and 0 candidate evaluations. The original selector returned 124 `candidate-not-marketable` and 1 `candidate-affordable`, with zero proposed or accepted signals in that window. The run summary records no `captureFailures`.

That makes **125 calls the absolute opportunity ceiling**, not the expected or proven saving. The evidence establishes **0 calls safely rejectable from the retained P13P evidence alone**, because it does not preserve the full per-call set of costs, selected/ranked candidates, route rows, and control authorization needed to prove that all possible current-signal paths are excluded. It also does not preserve a completeness certificate for every Market resource row. Some or many calls could become rejectable with current-cycle equivalence semantics, but the 4/121 first-missing-row counts do not tell how many: a later candidate may use a different observed resource, and the sole affordable result may still have other candidate paths. No new prefilter is justified from this evidence, and no value between 0 and 125 is claimed.

## Work and measurement

The P13N window records 125 full selector evaluations. Its report attributes 116.5 ms to catalog-capture elapsed instrumentation and 252.3 ms to selector elapsed instrumentation across the calls; these are elapsed diagnostics and not renderer CPU. P13O’s calibrated 364.6–366.8 ms injected synchronous work, roughly 2% of the ordinary renderer CPU total, did not produce a consistent CPU or TaskDuration response within the bounded run budget. It therefore cannot resolve the full allocator’s 2% gate.

Deterministic operation counts are adequate for this decision: per P13P call, the gate inspected one cost entry and fourteen Market resources, while the selector still built/evaluated its graph; across the window this is 1,875 added record checks and zero avoided graph/candidate evaluations. The graph planner’s hard bound is 256 actions and depth 16; exact actual node visits were not retained. An amplified pure-function benchmark would estimate component execution cost only, not whole-renderer attribution or a 2% budget, and is not necessary to choose whether to retain a zero-benefit test-only planner. No new timing run or replay was performed.

## Recommendation and stop point

Retire the Stage 4A per-Market-tick selector and its P13P prefilter line of experimentation. Keep the architectural distinction clear: native Research publication/execution owns offers and purchases; Market owns current route planning and controls; autonomous allocation, if pursued for the canonical plan, needs an explicit shared owner contract spanning those systems and additional resource-producing owners. The current test-only hint has not shown enough accepted allocation benefit to justify recurring full graph work, and the two apparent reuse/screening paths lack current ownership/freshness evidence.

This report changes no production code and does not authorize production integration or declare CPU readiness. Stop here for independent review; any later autonomous-allocation proposal should begin from this owner-level decision, not another increasingly specialized P13P gate.
